Abstract

Safe indoor operation requires autonomous tracking of objects that are not directly visible to the robot. Optical non-line-of-sight (NLoS) methods depend on ambient lighting and scene geometry or on weak multi-bounce returns that need long acquisitions. Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar offers an attractive alternative: walls act as reflectors for mmWave signals, and frequency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radars enable fast, practical measurement of range, angle, and velocity. However, the low resolution of these radars leads to spatially ambiguous observations. The Doppler information, although particularly sensitive to target motion, remains underexplored in indoor NLoS localization: existing methods emphasize spatial representations and either collapse or discard the Doppler dimension before learning. To leverage the complete Range–Azimuth–Doppler (RAD) information, we acquire the first indoor NLoS radar dataset that pairs sequential RAD measurements with synchronized position and velocity ground truth under controlled variations in motion: 576 sequences totaling 720,856 frames across 12 trajectories of a mobile robot moving along varying speeds, starting positions, and material configurations. We then formulate NLoS tracking as sequential position-velocity estimation rather than frame-wise localization: a shared 3-D encoder processes the full RAD representation of consecutive frames, separate recurrent branches estimate position and velocity, and post-inference refinement combines the two into a motion-consistent trajectory. Our method achieves 8.95 cm position and 1.75 cm/s velocity errors on held-out trajectories and generalizes to unseen scenarios involving a moving human target. Together, our dataset and tracker establish a benchmark for tracking hidden moving targets with a low-cost radar.

Dataset

We collect 576 sequences comprising 720,856 synchronized radar frames, pairing full RAD measurements with position and velocity ground truth. The dataset covers 12 trajectories, three nominal speeds (0.25, 0.75, and 1.0 m/s), four starting positions, and four target configurations.

The radar faces a relay wall, while the target moves outside its direct field of view. All recordings use wall-reflected signals.

Method

We formulate NLoS tracking as sequential position–velocity estimation from full RAD measurements. The framework consists of three stages:

  1. A shared 3-D residual CNN extracts features from each RAD frame, preserving range, azimuth, and Doppler information.
  2. Separate recurrent branches estimate position and velocity from the same feature sequence. Each branch combines a frame-level prediction with a learned temporal correction.
  3. Post-inference refinement combines predicted positions with velocity-based motion constraints to produce a motion-consistent trajectory and reduce stationary jitter and drift.
